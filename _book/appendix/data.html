Appendix B — The datasets

Every dataset in this booklet is simulated by scripts/make_data.R, from seed 2025. Run Rscript scripts/make_data.R from the project root to regenerate all of them; data/PROVENANCE.txt records when and with which R version.

Simulation is deliberate, for three reasons. The generating process is known, so every diagnostic question has a checkable answer. No information governance approval is needed, so the files can live in a public repository. And the defects are the ones we chose, so they can be taught.

B.1 los_data.csv — the training world

1000 admissions. Used in Exercises 1.2 and 1.3.

Column Meaning Generating process
Age years Normal(60, 15), truncated below at 18, not truncated above
Severity severity score, 1–10 Normal(5.4, 2.07), clipped to [1, 10]
LOS length of stay, days \(-8.5 + 0.94\,\text{Age} + 3.37\,\text{Severity} + \varepsilon\), \(\varepsilon \sim N(0, 4.3)\)

Known defects, retained on purpose.

  • The maximum age is just over 109, and 26 patients are aged 90 or more. Nothing truncates the upper tail, exactly as in extracts where a default or mistyped date of birth survives into the warehouse.
  • Typical LOS is about 66 days, which is wrong for acute medicine by an order of magnitude.
  • LOS is symmetric. Real length of stay is strongly right-skewed. This is the single most recognisable difference between this file and a real one.
  • Severity piles up at exactly 1 and 10 because the draw is clipped, not bounded: 2.0% of rows sit at 1 and 1.6% at 10.

Both of the first two are the subject of Exercise 1.2, Task 1.

These parameters match the los_data.csv distributed in earlier years of the module, so answers stated as ranges are unaffected by which copy a student has.

B.2 extract_A.csv, extract_B.csv, extract_C.csv — the later extracts

400 admissions each. Used in Exercise 1.3. Students are told only that they are monthly extracts taken after the model went live, and must diagnose what changed. The mechanisms are in scripts/make_data.R and in the instructor chapter.

WarningPublishing note

scripts/make_data.R contains the answer to Exercise 1.3. If you publish the student site from the same repository, either exclude scripts/ from the published output or accept that a determined student can read the key. Nothing else in the booklet gives it away.

B.3 los_data_skewed.csv — the realistic variant

1000 admissions, same predictors, but LOS is drawn from a Gamma distribution with a multiplicative mean, \(\mathbb{E}[\text{LOS}] = \exp(0.15 + 0.012\,\text{Age} + 0.17\,\text{Severity})\), shape 2. Median around four days, with a long right tail. Not used on Day 1; it exists for the sessions on metrics and on generalised linear models, where the difference between a symmetric and a skewed outcome changes the answer.

B.4 birthweight.csv — the transfer task

600 pregnancies. bpd (biparietal diameter, mm) and ad (abdominal diameter, mm) predict bw (birth weight, g) through \(-1700 + 30\,\text{bpd} + 25\,\text{ad} + \varepsilon\), \(\varepsilon \sim N(0, 250)\). Units and magnitudes are plausible; the numbers are invented. Used in Exercise 1.2 Task 4 so that students rerun the pipeline on columns whose names bear no resemblance to the ones they just used.

B.5 Using real data instead

Any of these can be swapped for a real dataset, and the booklet’s structure survives it, but two things change and should be said out loud when you do it. The known answer disappears, so the self-checks that compare against a key (check_shift_answers()) no longer apply; and the defects become the ones the extract happens to have rather than the ones the exercise needs. Teaching diagnosis first on data with a known answer, then on real data, is the order that works.
