clinic_extract.csv
  Simulated here, seed 20262. 180 rows, one per patient, as a deliberately
  messy GP extract. There is no bmi column on purpose: height and weight are
  given and BMI is derived by students in Exercise 2.3, which is what makes
  the three heights typed in metres matter - they propagate into a BMI in the
  hundreds of thousands against a plausible range of about 16 to 44.

  Deliberate defects: patient_id is unique per row, so one-hot encoding it
  gives one column per patient; GP_practice has four common practices, three
  rare ones and an Other that already exists, so grouping rare levels merges
  into an existing category and cuts the encoded columns from 16 to 13; sex
  appears as Male/male/M/Female/female/F and smoking_status in four
  spellings; age contains one impossible 250 and weight_kg one impossible
  800.

  Missing values use five conventions across four columns - the strings
  unknown, ?, not recorded and N/A, the number -999, and genuinely empty
  cells. A naive is.na() count finds 9 of the 44. -999 is the dangerous one:
  it parses as a number, so systolic_BP loads as numeric and its mean reads
  73.6 mmHg instead of 131.7 with no error and no warning, whereas the text
  codes at least force height_cm and weight_kg to character.

  diabetes was drawn from the complete values before anything was spoiled,
  and systolic_BP is a straight line in age and BMI, so Exercise 2.6 has a
  genuinely predictable regression target.

hospital_admissions.csv and creatinine_truth.csv
  250 simulated admissions, seed 20263, generated in scripts/make_data.R.
  Columns: patient_id, admission_type, age, height_cm, weight_kg, systolic_BP,
  heart_rate, creatinine, CRP, smoking_status, length_of_stay.

  creatinine (umol/L) is right-skewed - mean 78.9, median 76 - because renal
  impairment is made commoner with age. It is missing for 42 patients, more
  often in emergency admissions (30 of 134) than elective (12 of 116), and for
  no other reason: missing at random given admission type. Age and heart rate
  carry real signal about it, so a regression-based imputer beats the mean,
  but most of the variation is unexplained, so it still misses.

  creatinine_truth.csv holds the true value for 18 of those 42 patients. Mean
  absolute error against it: mean imputation 11.44, median 11.33, mice with
  norm.predict 8.45, scikit-learn IterativeImputer 8.35. No patient is
  recovered exactly; the worst is H151, true 92, imputed about 113.

  smoking_status is missing for 32 patients, 27 of them emergency admissions,
  so the missingness is itself informative. Filling with the mode moves Never
  from 42.8% of the cohort to 55.6%.

  CRP (mg/L) is complete and log-normal: median 10.65, maximum 264.6, skew 4.43
  falling to 0.11 after a log. It is higher in emergencies and in longer stays,
  and was drawn last so that adding it changed no other value. Min-max scaling
  leaves 79% of patients below 0.1.

  length_of_stay (days) is log-normal, the skewed target for later exercises.
  Dropping incomplete rows keeps 182 of 250 and takes emergency admissions from
  53.6% of the data to 45.6%.
